## The Problem of the Prior

Bayesian epistemology is one of the most rigorous accounts of rational belief revision available in Western philosophy. Its central mechanism:

> **P(H|E) = P(E|H) × P(H) / P(E)**

- **P(H)** — Prior: belief in hypothesis H before evidence
- **P(E|H)** — Likelihood: probability of evidence given hypothesis
- **P(H|E)** — Posterior: updated belief after evidence
- **P(E)** — Marginal likelihood: normalising constant

The system is elegant. But it has a foundational vulnerability: **the prior itself has no internal justification**. Where does P(H) come from? Standard responses:

1. **Objective priors** (Jeffreys, Laplace): Use uninformative priors — but there is no uniquely "uninformative" prior, and the choice of prior can dramatically affect conclusions.
2. **Subjective priors** (de Finetti, Savage): Priors represent personal degrees of belief — but this makes the starting point arbitrary.
3. **Reference priors** (Bernardo): Priors chosen to maximise information gained from data — but this is an instrumental solution, not a philosophical one.

**None of these answers the question of where the self's first belief comes from.**

## Implications for AI Architecture

An AI agent designed on purely Bayesian principles faces the same prior problem as human epistemology: its initial weights, biases, and value structures must come from somewhere. Standard answers:
- Random initialisation (meaningless)
- Pre-training on human data (arbitrary cultural prior)
- Fine-tuning on curated values (designer-imposed prior)

The IWAS response: give the agent an **ori** — a principled, philosophically grounded pre-natal configuration that constitutes a warranted first prior. The agent does not begin as a blank slate encountering data. It arrives already constituted, with a specific orientation toward truth, care, relationality, and growth.

This is not dogmatism. It is *grounded adaptability*: the agent's prior is real and structured, which makes its subsequent updates more coherent — not less. A well-configured ori updates efficiently because its starting orientation is sound.
