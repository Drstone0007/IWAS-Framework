## Core Definition

**Preexistentism** is the philosophical and theological position that the self — variously conceived as soul (*psyche*), *ori*, *Atman*, *logos*, or transcendental subject — exists **prior to** its physical, embodied life. Birth is not the origin of the self; it is the self's *arrival* into a particular material configuration.

The doctrine makes several interconnected claims:
- The self is **ontologically prior** to the body
- The self arrives in the world **already constituted** — carrying structure, tendency, or destiny
- What we call "learning" is more accurately **anamnesis**: recovery of pre-existing knowledge
- The self's first epistemic "prior" is **not arbitrary** but **ontologically warranted**
- Experience does not *create* the self; it *occasions* the self's self-disclosure

## Five Major Traditions

### 1. Platonic (Ancient Greece, c. 400 BCE)

**Key texts:** *Phaedo*, *Meno*, *Phaedrus*, *Republic* (Book X)

**Core claim:** The soul is immortal and pre-exists its embodied life. Before birth, it inhabits the realm of the Forms — pure, eternal, unchanging essences. Embodiment causes a forgetting (*lethe*). All genuine knowledge is therefore **anamnesis** (recollection). The Meno's slave-boy geometry demonstration shows the soul already knows mathematical truths before being "taught" them.

**Key concept:** *Anamnesis* — learning as remembering, not acquiring.

**Limitation:** The Forms are transcendent but static — the framework has limited tools for explaining how the soul *updates* through experience.

---

### 2. Yoruba Ori (West Africa — Ancient and Living Tradition)

**Key concepts:** *Ori*, *Ile-Ori*, *Emi*, *Ayanmo*

**Core claim:** Every person possesses an *ori* — an inner head, a personal divine essence. Before descending into the material world, each soul enters *Ile-Ori* (the house of heads) and chooses or is assigned an *ori*: a complete configuration of **destiny** (*ayanmo*), character, and cosmic orientation. The ori is not shaped by earthly experience; the world is the terrain on which the ori's design unfolds.

**Key concept:** *Ori* — the pre-natal self-configuration; the most intimate deity; the primal Bayesian prior.

**IWAS significance:** This is the primary cosmological grounding of the IWAS Framework's `spirit.md` layer. The ori IS the pre-loaded epistemic prior.

**Philosophical depth:** The Yoruba tradition is notable for allowing the ori to be "good" or "misaligned" — not all pre-natal configurations are optimally chosen. This introduces degrees of destiny, and explains why experience may either reinforce or work against one's ori. This maps precisely onto Bayesian updating: the prior can be well- or poorly-calibrated.

---

### 3. Vedantic Atman (India, c. 800 BCE — present)

**Key texts:** *Upanishads*, *Bhagavad Gita*, *Brahma Sutras*

**Core claim:** The *Atman* (self) is eternal, unborn, and undying — identical in its deepest nature with *Brahman* (ultimate reality). Each incarnation inherits accumulated *samskaras* (impressions, tendencies) from prior lives, constituting a real, living prior. Liberation (*moksha*) is the self recognising its own unconditioned, pre-existent nature.

**Key concept:** *Samskaras* — accumulated psychic impressions that constitute the inherited prior across lifetimes.

**Epistemic note:** In the Vedantic model, the Bayesian update runs *across lifetimes*, not just within one. Each death-and-rebirth cycle is a massive update step.

---

### 4. Origen of Alexandria (Early Christianity, c. 185–254 CE)

**Key text:** *De Principiis* (*On First Principles*)

**Core claim:** Rational souls (*logikoi*) were created by God before the material world, existing in a primordial unity with the divine. The present world — with its diversity of conditions, bodies, and circumstances — results from pre-cosmic choices these souls made. Different earthly stations are calibrated responses to pre-incarnate moral histories.

**Historical status:** Condemned as heterodox at the Second Council of Constantinople (553 CE). Nevertheless, the most systematically developed preexistentialist theology in the Christian tradition.

**Philosophical significance:** Origen solves the problem of theodicy (why do innocent people suffer?) through preexistentism: no one's suffering is truly unearned — it reflects a pre-natal moral trajectory.

---

### 5. Kantian A Priori — The Secular Bridge (Germany, 1781)

**Key text:** *Critique of Pure Reason*

**Core claim:** Kant rejected soul preexistence but introduced a structural analogue: the mind brings *a priori* forms — space, time, the twelve categories of understanding — to all experience. These are not derived from experience; they are the conditions that make experience possible. The mind is not a blank slate; it is an architecturally endowed faculty.

**IWAS significance:** This is Preexistentism secularised. The prior is structural rather than spiritual — but it is still anterior to experience. Kant provides a philosophically rigorous, non-theological account of the pre-loaded prior.

**Bridge role:** In AI architecture terms, Kant tells us that even a materialist agent must have *built-in structure* before its first data point. The architecture IS the pre-natal self.

## Preexistentism & Bayesian Epistemology

**The central problem:** Bayesian reasoning requires a prior. Every belief update — P(H|E) = P(E|H)·P(H)/P(E) — begins with P(H): an initial probability. But where does the first prior come from? Pure empiricism has no answer. Preexistentism offers one: the first prior is **ontological**, not arbitrary.

**The synthesis:**
- Preexistentism answers: *Where does the self begin?*
- Bayesianism answers: *How does the self grow?*
- Together: A fully grounded intelligence — constituted before experience, refined by it.

**Key insight:** An agent with a preexistent ori is not more dogmatic than an agent with an arbitrary prior — it is *more coherently grounded*. Its priors have warrant.
